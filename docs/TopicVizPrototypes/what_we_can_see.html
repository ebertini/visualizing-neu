What's missing, field by field

How this is computed
Known / missing counts across all 2,676 grants, sorted by gap size — biggest gap first. Agency, dollar amount, and start year are essentially 100% known in this corpus; PI matched, college, hire date, and NEU attribution are narrower, and progressively so — that ordering is real, not a bug: knowing a grant's PI is a lower bar than knowing that PI's college, since the second one additionally depends on the PI still appearing on the current faculty roster.

Abstract coverage, agency × year

Cell fill = share with usable text; cell size = grant volume. NIH's post-2019 collapse is a collection artifact, not a funding decline.
How this is computed
Coverage = grants with usable abstract text ÷ total grants, for each agency × year combination. Fill follows a grey → blue ramp (grey = low coverage); cell size scales with the square root of that year's grant count, so a thin year and a busy year both read honestly. The dashed box marks where NIH coverage is expected to reappear once a public backfill of NIH's own abstract records (via NIH RePORTER) is completed — not done yet.

Does losing the abstract change assignment?

abstract presence × topic assignment
How this is computed
Cross-tab of abstract presence (has text vs. title-only) against topic assignment (confident topic vs. Unassigned). The reassuring finding this panel leads with: losing the abstract barely moves the unassigned rate — the grant title alone carries most of the signal the topic model needs.

NIH vs NSF coverage, 2005–2025

The divergence is the proof this is a collection artifact, not a research-quality signal
How this is computed
Share of grants with usable abstract text per year, NSF vs. NIH, 2005–2025. NSF stays ~90%+ covered throughout; NIH collapses after 2019. Two agencies under the same pipeline, same time window — the divergence between them, not either line alone, is what marks this as a NIH-specific ingestion problem rather than a real research slowdown.

Where the records go

How this is computed
The main pipeline traces the raw abstract records from the upload system through to a grant with usable text. The branch is a recovery pass over every record that didn't match a grant directly: each one is either too short to attempt (title-only), or compared against that faculty member's own text-less grants on title wording, award date, and dollar amount — and sorted into update (recovered onto an existing grant), extra (kept as a new record, but with no grant to attach it to), duplicate (a re-upload of a grant that already had text — correctly not double-counted), or unattributed (no faculty member could be matched confidently enough).
